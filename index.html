Five officially adopted instruments form the formal architecture for employment, skills and career guidance in Kenya.

Active / operational Partially implemented

Kenya Vision 2030

Est. 2007 · Ministry of Planning

The overarching 25-year development blueprint. Employment and skills are embedded in the social and economic pillars, delivered through Medium Term Plans.

Active — MTP IV underway

Sessional Paper No. 4 of 2013

Est. 2013 · Ministry of Labour

Foundational employment policy establishing the decent work agenda. Most institutional bodies recommended within it remain unestablished a decade later.

Partially implemented

National Skills Development Policy

Est. 2023 · Ministry of Labour

Addresses the skills mismatch between education and the labour market. Implementation framework published; the proposed Skills Council has not yet been formed.

Policy active, council pending

National Policy Framework on Career Guidance

Est. 2023 · Ministry of Labour

Professionalises career counselling. Digital quarterly reporting live on Kenya Skills Hub. Career Guidance Council proposed but not yet gazetted.

Guidelines active, council pending

Public Service Internship Policy

Est. 2016 · Public Service Commission

Operational framework governing government-led internship placements. Active but limited to the public sector, leaving a private sector work-based learning gap.

Active — public sector only

National Employment Authority
NEA
EstablishedApril 2016
Enabling lawNEA Act No. 3 of 2016
UnderMinistry of Labour & Social Protection
TypeStatutory authority
Created from the former National Employment Bureau. Registers job seekers and employers, links candidates to opportunities, provides labour market information, offers vocational guidance and policy advisory. Obligated to facilitate student internship placements in both public and private sectors.

National Industrial Training Authority
NITA
Established2011 (revised 2012)
Enabling lawIndustrial Training Act Cap 237
UnderMinistry of Labour & Social Protection
TypeState corporation
Promotes quality and efficiency in industrial training across Kenya. Collects industrial training levies, develops curricula, accredits training institutions, awards Government Trade Test certificates, and integrates labour market information into skills development. Operates five industrial training centres nationwide.
nita.go.ke Operational
TVET Authority
TVETA
Established2013 (board: April 2014)
Enabling lawTVET Act No. 29 of 2013
UnderMinistry of Education
TypeState corporation
Regulates and coordinates all TVET training in Kenya through licensing, registration and accreditation of programmes, institutions and trainers. Successor to the Directorate of Technical Accreditation and Quality Assurance. Placed under the Ministry of Education's State Dept for TVET via Executive Order No. 1 of 2023.

Kenya National Qualifications Authority
KNQA
Established2015
Enabling lawKNQF Act No. 22 of 2014
UnderMinistry of Education
TypeStatutory authority
Coordinates and harmonises all qualification levels across Kenya's education and training system. Accredits Qualification Awarding Bodies (universities, polytechnics, professional bodies), maintains the National Qualifications Database, and develops the Kenya National Qualifications Framework (KNQF) for international comparability.
knqa.go.ke Operational

NPCC
Established2016 (renamed)
BasisExecutive Order No. 1 of 2016

Proposed institutional bodies

National Skills Development Council

Proposed in 2023 Skills Policy

High-level body to coordinate training across ministries. Requires the NSDC Act to be enacted first. No council formed as of 2026.

Awaiting legislation

National Career Guidance Council

Multi-stakeholder advisory body to lead and professionalise career guidance nationally. Requires a Career Guidance Act.

Sector-specific bodies aligning curricula to industry needs in ICT, manufacturing, hospitality etc. Pre-conditional on NSDC being established.

Several themes recur simultaneously across multiple policies and proposed instruments — creating both coordination potential and fragmentation risk.

Skills mismatch between education and labour market

All five established policies name this as a root problem. Three of the proposed institutions are specifically designed to resolve it — yet no single coordinating body is operational, so the diagnosis is widely shared while the remedy remains fragmented across multiple ministries.

Vision 2030Sessional Paper 4, 2013Skills Dev. Policy 2023NSDC (proposed)Sector Skills Councils (proposed)

School-to-work transition

Named explicitly in both the 2013 Employment Policy and the 2023 Career Guidance Framework. The Public Service Internship Policy addresses a small slice. The gap for private sector work-based learning has been called out across two policy cycles and remains unresolved in 2026.

Sessional Paper 4, 2013Career Guidance Framework 2023PS Internship Policy 2016Broad Internship Policy (proposed)

Institutional coordination vs. fragmentation

Every major policy acknowledges that responsibilities are split across too many ministries with no overarching coordination body. Ironically, the proposed solution — four new councils and two new Acts — risks compounding the fragmentation if not sequenced carefully or housed under a single ministry mandate.

Sessional Paper 4, 2013Skills Dev. Policy 2023Career Guidance Framework 2023NSDC (proposed)NPCC (partial)Career Guidance Council (proposed)

Professionalisation of career services

Both the 2013 paper and the 2023 Career Guidance Framework call for career guidance to be treated as a formal profession with standards, licensing and training. The 2023 framework goes further with a proposed Council and Act — but the legal infrastructure is not yet in place.

Sessional Paper 4, 2013Career Guidance Framework 2023Career Guidance Council (proposed)Career Guidance Act (proposed)

Productivity as a policy lever

Vision 2030 and the 2013 Sessional Paper both anchor productivity as central to economic growth. A decade later only a government department (NPCC) exists in place of the proposed statutory council — and the enabling bill remains at stakeholder validation stage. The longest-standing implementation gap in the landscape.
